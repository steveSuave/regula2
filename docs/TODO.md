## Phase 120c — Three defects from user documents (conics era)

Opened 2026-08-17 from `~/Documents/geometry/regula/debug`: conics drawing as polygons (`square-ellipse.rgl`, `square-hyperbola.rgl`), intersection points accumulating into double points (`ellipse-intersection-issue.rgl`), and a there-and-back drag randomly returning a crossing on the wrong side (`cinderella-jumping.rgl`). All three are engine defects reachable only now that a kind can produce a general conic, or only on a gesture nobody had measured.

- [x] **The conic walk gave up on depth before it met its tolerance.** `ConicShape.polylines` bounds bisection two ways, and only `maxSamples` is a cost cap — spending `maxDepth` emits the chord however far the curve strays. The sweep parameter is a pencil angle whose map to arc length spreads `|dX/dφ|` by ~1e7 on ordinary figures (~24 bisections), so the cap of 12 bound: 10 px of sagitta against a half-pixel tolerance on the saved ellipse, 172 px on a hyperbola branch, neither anywhere near `maxSamples` at 4000 *or* 200000. Raised to 32, with the distinction between the two bounds written down; all 32 goldens byte-identical (their conics sit near the origin, where 12 sufficed — which is why Phase 119 missed it)
- [x] **Branch adoption collapsed four conic crossings onto one.** `_traceAlong` wrote `matchedIndex` back to `branchIndex` only for `matchedIndex <= 1` — correct while two candidates was all any pair could produce, left behind when Phase 120 made four reachable (`maxBranchCount` had already been raised for the same reason). The cap does not merely skip adoption: it makes it **asymmetric**, so a root landing at 0 or 1 writes back while one at 2 or 3 does not, two branches converge onto one stored index, and the next static recompute puts both points on the same root. Three of four points collapsed within two frames of replaying the reported document. Cap now tracks `maxBranchCount`
- [x] **`IntersectionTool` stacked duplicates on branches it could not see.** Dedup classified existing points by proximity, which can only speak for a point that *has* a position, so while the crossings were complex every tap looked unoccupied. Added a structural test — an `IntersectionPoint` on the same ordered pair at the same index is that intersection by construction — which dedups defined or not
- [x] **`setIntersectionBranch` carried a third `0..1` bound** — and it is the one path a *committed* re-pointing takes, so a traced drag adopting index 2 or 3 threw out of `MoveFreePointCommand.apply`, the re-pointing was discarded, and the crossings went on merging in the app after the engine had stopped merging them. Found only by testing at session level; the first round's tests drove `Construction.recomputeAlongPath` directly and never touched the command. Every bound now derives from `maxBranchCount` (PLAN, same section)
- [x] **A round trip is honest: the drag detour half-plane is now a constant** (`dragDetourOrientation`; PLAN §"A round trip is honest"). Dragging two curves apart through a tangency and back now *trades* the two crossings, and doing it again trades them back — the monodromy of the root split, and Cinderella's behaviour. This replaces reversal-identity (Phases 115–120b), **on the user's call**: that convention's observable hid a real feature of the geometry, and obtaining it required reading the drag direction, where any ±1 rule has a seam — that one's lay on the horizontal axis, so a twentieth of a pixel of `dy` jitter flipped the outcome about a third of the time. A constant reads nothing about the gesture and has no seam. `detourOrientation` deleted; the alternation machinery tried first (`IntersectionPoint.lastDetourOrientation`) deleted with it
- [x] Tests: sagitta measured against the true curve on both saved figures (plus the depth-12 discriminator, and a pin that the sample budget is not the binding bound); four conic∩conic branches keeping four distinct addresses across a drag through vanishing crossings, **and the same through `DragSession` + `end()` + `apply()`** with undo restoring the pre-drag indices; an occupied-but-undefined branch refusing a tap; one round trip trading the crossings and two restoring them, on the free-point drive and the parameter drive, with the outcome independent of jitter (three levels × 20 seeds) and of frame coarseness; the Phase 115 and 116b there-and-back tests flipped from identity to monodromy
- [x] **Two intersection points may never share a branch** (PLAN §"Two intersection points may never share a branch"), defended at all three routes rather than the one that happened to fail: the tool refuses an occupied branch, adoption is **atomic per pair** so partial adoption can never leave a stale index colliding with a fresh one, and the decoder separates duplicates a pre-120c build wrote, two-pass so a duplicate is never moved onto a crossing a later point holds, reporting the ids on `DecodedDocument.repairedIntersections`
- [x] **The pair is stored in canonical order — the defect's last root cause.** Reproduced from the user's screen recording (`~/Movies/2026-08-17_17-58-54.mkv`): the two lower crossings of two bifocal ellipses go complex under a focus drag and come back **stacked**, leaving the fourth crossing vacant to be tapped afresh. Every rig built on one parent order had passed — 1176 loop drags, 770 two-gesture round trips, coarse frames down to one per gesture, bails firing throughout. Mixing tap order broke it on the first try: 1237 of 3078. `branchIndex` addresses the canonical order of `intersectionCandidates(curve1, curve2)`, the reversed pair is a different order, and *not by a fixed permutation* — reversing flips the real-finite tier only, so the correspondence moves as candidates go real or complex. Two points on opposite orderings are therefore two address spaces and every guard above compares within one. `IntersectionPoint` is now a factory that stores the pair id-ascending whichever way it is named, renumbering the requested branch onto that order by chordal match; the tool's cross-order machinery and the decoder's reference-ordering machinery both delete as dead. Behaviour change: branch *labels* mirror for pairs that used to be stored reversed (positions do not), which is what the six relabelled expectations in the Cinderella and corpus tests record
- [x] Tests: the recorded sequence itself — tap three crossings in mixed tap orders, drag them away and back, tap the vacancy, drag again — asserting no two points ever share a crossing and no two share a branch; plus canonical storage and renumbering on a reversed tap. Both fail on the pre-fix tree
- [x] Perf gate re-run (VM + dart2js + dart2wasm): PASS at 2% / 10% of budget, checksums identical — the benchmark takes no detours, so the new path is not on its hot loop

## Phase 121 — Old kernel deletion + convention unification

- [x] ~~Revisit the detour orientation convention~~ — **settled in Phase 120c**: drags take a constant half-plane and a round trip carries honest monodromy (PLAN §"A round trip is honest"). Was deferred from 117 on a user decision of 2026-08-14 and reopened by the user on 2026-08-17
- [x] Unify the last convention non-uniformity it left: **locus** runs used `detourOrientation1D` (odd in the drive direction) while drags used the constant. Unified onto one `detourHalfPlane` (renamed from `dragDetourOrientation`, which is no longer drag-only; `detourOrientation1D` deleted), and the fixture diffs are **nil, bitwise** — measured on all four locus documents, of which only `apatitos-topos.rgl` detours at all, twice, both on forward legs, which is exactly where the two rules disagreed. The reason generalizes and is now recorded (PLAN, same section): an arc encircles its one estimated singularity by a half-turn whichever side it passes, and a half-turn either way exchanges the two sheets of a simple branch point — so the half-plane can only matter when an arc encloses more than one collision, or one of higher order, where the arc is mis-planned and neither rule is right. New coverage pins that the Phase 117b rig *detours* (two arcs, no folds), so the sheet assertions over it are known to exercise one
- [x] `construction.dart` carried a **literal NUL byte** in a composite map key (`'${curve1.id}\x00${curve2.id}'`, the Phase 120c per-pair adoption group), which made `file(1)` call it `data` and `grep`/`rg` skip the whole file as binary — silently, so a search for anything in the largest domain file came back empty. Escaped; same string value, and the file is text again
- [x] `locus.dart`'s index-flip consistency guard assumed two candidates (`old.length != 2 || now.length != 2 → skip`), so a conic∩conic pair inside a locus chain silently lost that check rather than crashing. **Widened**, and moved to `trace_acceptance.dart` as `relabelIsBenign` where it is directly testable: a flip is a benign canonical relabel exactly when every root the slot did *not* follow pairs with a distinct other root across the flip within the same motion allowance. Nearest-match is exact rather than greedy here because the cap is at most half the set's minimum pairwise separation, so no pairing is ambiguous; at two candidates the general form reduces to the old `1 - index` arithmetic exactly. Unequal candidate counts still impose nothing — a root has entered or left the real set and the walk's other rules own that
- [x] Delete `lib/domain/math/intersections.dart` — **moved** to `test/v1_oracle/intersections.dart` rather than deleted, and with it the three other V1 modules that turned out to be in the same state (`angle_bisector`, `circle_relations`, `harmonic`, `tangents`: no `lib/` importer since Phase 110 superseded each with a projective kernel). Deleting them would have deleted the *evidence* that the projective kernels still agree with V1 on real transverse cases, which is the contract `branchIndex` addresses and is permanent because v1 documents are. Six test files consume them as oracles. Living under `test/` turns the standing rule from a grep gate into the directory layout, and `layer_rule_test.dart` gained a gate that no shipped file reaches into `test/`
- [x] Demote `LineEq`/`CircleEq` to view structs (remove throwing construction paths from domain flows) — already true in code and now stated where it is read: nothing constructs either outside `lib/domain/`, and inside it only through `toLineEq`/`toCircleEq` or behind a guard, so the throwing factories are a programmer-error contract rather than a path a recompute can fall down. The one exception was `InscribedCircle`, which passed `inradius(...)!` straight into `CircleEq` on the strength of two helpers sharing a collinearity test; it now checks. Recorded in both type headers and CLAUDE.md, whose "legacy affine math (frozen; shrinking)" row was no longer true of what is left in `lib/domain/math/`
- [x] One degeneracy convention everywhere: projective value total; projection nullable — audited across all 44 kinds and found already uniform (`null` only when a parent's projective value is null, or when the computed homogeneous value is the **zero triple**, which is not a projective object). Stated at the declaration site in `geo_object.dart` with its three sanctioned exceptions named (`IntersectionPoint`'s realness gate, `PointOnObject` without a chart, the Phase 112 consumer kinds), and given a gate: collapsing every free point in the two kitchen-sink corpora onto one position produces all 44 degeneracies at once, and every surviving affine view must still have an agreeing projective value (29 do — a degeneracy is mostly a value, not an absence)
- [x] Remove lift-default dead code where every kind now overrides — `GeoPoint.projPoint`, `GeoLine.projLine` and `GeoCircle.conic` are **abstract**. All 44 kinds already implemented them (eight only through a migrated intermediate base — `TriangleCenterPoint`, `RelativeLine`, `TriangleCircle` — which is why a per-file grep undercounts), as did every test stub: the change is analyze-clean and suite-green with nothing else touched. The defaults were right during the migration and are a trap after it, because a kind that forgot to store homogeneous state would silently inherit a lift of its affine value instead of failing to compile. The `lift` constructors themselves stay: they are the chart → homogeneous kernel API, not scaffolding
- [x] `point_coincidence.dart` re-pointed at projected positions — **it needed no code change, which is the finding**: the probe only reads `GeoPoint.position` and writes `FreePoint` positions and `PointOnObject` parameters, all of which are projections and chart parameters now, so the perturbation still perturbs exactly the mutable roots and the restore is still bit-exact (a `FreePoint` stores the lift of its position, `w` exactly 1). Two consequences are deliberate and now stated in the file and pinned by a test: an off-chart point (at infinity, or complex) has no position and is never merged — two pairs of parallels meeting at the *same* projective point stay two points, the conservative branch this function takes everywhere — and the tolerance stays in world units, because it screens "one dot or two", a question about the chart and not a projective invariant
- [x] Grep gates recorded in CLAUDE.md; PLAN's migration-contract section replaced by the final architecture description. PLAN §"Migration strategy" is now §"Architecture" — same five numbered points, rewritten as what the migration arrived at, with the rejected alternatives kept so they are not re-litigated. Two of the gates stopped being greps and became structure: `test/v1_oracle/` cannot be imported from `lib/` (and `layer_rule_test.dart` checks it), and the projective accessors are abstract, so a kind cannot forget its homogeneous state. Migration-era wording ("migrated kind", "unmigrated objects") retired from CLAUDE.md and `geo_object.dart`
