## Phase 151 — M-P2d: the incidence closure (done)

The first of the two structural gaps Phase 150 found behind its three rules (PLAN §"Two closures the rule table is standing in for"). Ahead of M-P3 in value, and the cheapest of the three open prover phases.

**Split, and the split is what caught the error.** 151a landed the closure with no consumer; 151b was to move the matcher and delete the rules it subsumed. Measured separately, the matcher move turned out to be a **regression on every fixture** — had it shipped together with three rule deletions, the cause would have been unreadable. 151c then chased the fact key instead, and a third measurement closed that too: **the closure's consumer is M-P3.** So the phase ships its structure and hands the rest to Phase 152, which is now next.

### 151a — the carrier index (done)

- [x] **Lines and circles as objects carrying their point sets** (`lib/domain/prover/carriers.dart`), merged by union-find over the tuples that name them — pairs for a line, triples for a circle, which is the classical soundness argument and the only difference between the two closures. A `Fact` keeps its point-tuple form: the vocabulary and the save format do not move, and nothing here writes to a `FactDatabase`
- [x] **The invariant that makes a lookup a map read**: every arity-subset of a class's point set maps to that class, maintained by re-keying after each merge. So a merge that makes two previously unrelated names equal is found when the points are re-keyed, not later by a rule — and `lineThrough` is total, a pair no `coll` has mentioned answering the carrier of exactly those two points
- [x] **Incremental, because the engine is**: `absorb` takes one fact at a time and lands where a rebuild from the whole database lands, in any arrival order (pinned both ways)
- [x] **Measured against the rule set as it stands**, before anything moved — and read too optimistically at the time, see 151b. On `perp-true-unproved.rgl` (+ its auxiliary point) 8 `coll` facts are 2 line carriers of 4 points each, and `coll_transitive` + `perp_coll` account for 9 of the 41 facts; on `provoleas2.json` 11 `coll` facts are 2 carriers (5 and 4 points) and the two rules account for 11 of 75. **A full closure rebuild over `provoleas2`'s 75 facts costs 41 µs** — less than one average rule application (Session 158 measured 65 µs to 2.3 ms). The closure is cheap; what 151b then found is that cheapness was never the binding constraint
- [x] **`cyclic_fifth_point` fires on no fixture**, so the circle closure has no measured consumer today — recorded as `para_coll`'s status was, rather than claimed as a saving
- [x] Tests (`test/domain/prover/carriers_test.dart`, 19): the empty closure's total lookup; merge on a shared name and *no* merge on a shared point (a pencil is not a line); the merge only the re-keying finds; `coll_transitive`'s and `cyclic_fifth_point`'s content as unions; what it refuses (`para`/`perp`/`midp`, a repeated point); incremental = rebuilt, forwards and backwards; and a sweep over a real run asserting every `coll` the rule derived is already a lookup in the closure built from the facts it did *not* derive
- [x] Gates: analyze clean, suite **3003** green (2984 + 19)

### 151b — where the closure goes, settled by measurement (done — as a negative)

The step that was going to move the matcher, and the measurement that says not to. **Nothing shipped but the table and the finding**, which is the `para_coll` precedent from Phase 150: written, rigged, measured, deleted, recorded.

- [x] **The slot-shape table** (`lineSlots` in `carriers.dart`): which argument pairs name a *line* and may therefore be resolved through the closure. `para`/`perp` both pairs, `eqangle` all four (it reads mod π); `cong`/`eqratio` **none**, because a pair there names a segment and a segment has a length its carrier does not. Pinned against `Predicate.holdsOn` over a seeded sweep, driven off the table rather than restating it — mutation-checked: listing `cong`'s pairs fails on a numeric counterexample. Kept, because it is correct and is the substrate whichever way the design goes
- [x] **The carrier-aware matcher was built, flagged and measured — and reverted.** Resolving a premise's line slots through the closure, rule table unchanged: `locus3` **162 applications and quiescent → 4 000 000 and still running** (35 s), 29 facts against 15; `apatitos-topos` 632 apps / 1.3 s → past 30 000 / 11.7 s. Removing `coll_transitive` / `perp_coll` / `cyclic_fifth_point` on top *loses* facts — 16 on `perp-true-unproved.rgl` including the `perp` the JGEX proof turns on, 32 on `provoleas2.json`
- [x] **The mechanism, which is the reusable part.** Identity matching *prunes*: a premise bound to the wrong point fails at once and its join subtree is never walked. Carrier matching removes the pruning while the conclusion side still emits point tuples, so every newly-successful join yields another *spelling*, which is stored, which pivots, which joins more loosely still. On the premise side alone the closure is a fact multiplier — "cheaper to match" is not "fewer matches"
- [x] **PLAN §"Two closures…" amended first**, since the sentence being corrected ("deletes rules, deletes the spelling search, makes every later rule cheaper") is a PLAN claim
- [x] **`coll_transitive` and `perp_coll` stay.** They are the patch, and the patch is measurably cheaper than the half-closure that was going to replace it. `questions.dart`'s all-spellings search stays with them

### 151c — the carrier-reduced fact key (closed, unbuilt: M-P3 is the consumer)

What M-P2d looked like it wanted after 151b: `perp(A,B,C,D)` and `perp(E,F,C,D)` as one database entry. Three measurements, all taken off a baseline run with no engine change, closed it instead.

- [x] **The upside is real and concentrated.** `provoleas2.json` — the fixture that never reaches quiescence — holds **53 line-shaped facts that are 8 distinct statements**; its 75 facts reduce to 30, and `perp-true-unproved.rgl` 41 → 28. The three fixtures that already converge collapse by **nothing**
- [x] **The rule table cannot take a plain reduced key.** 10 of 26 rules have a variable in both a line role and a point-or-segment role (`perp_bisector`, `inscribed_angle` and its converse, `isosceles_base` and its converse, `midline_para`, `intercept_eqratio`, `sas_simtri`, `aa_simtri`, `perp_coll`). `perp_bisector` — `cong(o,a,o,b) & cong(p,a,p,b) => perp(o,p,a,b)` — binds `a`, `b` as segment ends and then names them as a line. A design that survives it is narrower: one entry per carrier-statement *carrying its witness spellings*, enumerating only at spanning variables
- [x] **And then the third measurement made it moot.** Split the line-shaped facts by kind: `provoleas2` is para 18 / perp 11 / eqangle 24, `perp-true-unproved` 9 / 12 / 3, `apatitos-topos` 0 / 1 / 13. **Every line-shaped kind is a kind AR absorbs** — and that is the same observation from the other end, since `lineSlots` is non-empty for exactly the predicates whose arguments name *lines*, which is what θ is a variable over. After M-P3, `para`/`perp`/`eqangle` are not facts, and `lineSlots` is empty for every kind that remains. A witness-set database would deduplicate facts M-P3 deletes
- [x] **So M-P2d's consumer is M-P3, not a fact-key redesign**, and PLAN's §M-P3 bullet says so. The ordering was backwards: incidence went before AR because it looked cheaper, and it *is* cheaper — but only the half that was already free (Phase 151a). `coll_transitive`, `perp_coll` and `questions.dart`'s all-spellings search stay until AR lands

## Phase 152 — M-P3: the angle and ratio algebra (open)

The second structural gap, and the real M-P3 (PLAN §M-P3, rewritten). Not a second rule engine — a linear system. After Phase 151 it is also M-P2d's consumer: θ is indexed per *carrier*, so `carriers.dart` is what it is built on.

**The arithmetic was settled before any code** — PLAN §"AR is a ℤ-module, not a ℚ-vector space". θ is defined mod π, so a linear form is a statement only when its coefficients are **integers**; every equation the vocabulary produces starts at ±1, but elimination does not keep them there. Two ordinary `eqangle` facts — `∠(l₁,l₂) = ∠(l₃,l₄)` and `∠(l₁,l₂) = ∠(l₄,l₃)` — sum to `2θ₂ − 2θ₁ = 0`, which over ℚ pivots to `para(l₁,l₂)` and over ℤ says only `∠(l₁,l₂) ∈ {0, π/2}`: parallel **or** perpendicular. Rational elimination turns a disjunction into a theorem, and the numeric filter would wave it through on any diagram sitting on the parallel branch. So: integer row reduction, entailment is ℤ-span membership, a `2θ` row publishes **nothing**, and log-lengths are a separate genuinely-ℚ system.

### 152a — the arithmetic and the closure, pure (done)

- [x] **`Rational`** (`lib/domain/prover/rational.dart`) — exact, `BigInt`-backed. `int` is not the same type on every target this ships to (64-bit on the VM and under dart2wasm, a double-backed 53-bit integer under dart2js), so an int-backed coefficient would overflow *differently* per platform and the browser gate would find it late. Canonical form — positive denominator, coprime — so `==` is value equality and `hashCode` agrees. `modOne` is the ℚ/ℤ reduction the angle constants live in, and `perp` twice over coming back to `0` is `perp_perp_para` arriving as arithmetic
- [x] **The angle closure as a ℤ-module** (`angle_closure.dart`): `AngleEquation` is `Σ cᵥθᵥ ≡ k (mod π)` with **integer** coefficients and the constant in units of π; `AngleClosure` keeps rows in echelon form by *lattice* reduction — never dividing a row by its own leading coefficient, combining two rows through the extended gcd instead. `entails` is ℤ-span membership, and the divisibility test is literally where ℚ and ℤ part company
- [x] **Provenance from day one**, the `FactDatabase` precedent: every row carries the integer combination of *input* equations it is, `entails` answers with that certificate, and `recombine` multiplies it back out — so verifying an AR step is arithmetic, not search. Pinned by a sweep: whatever a tangled closure claims to prove, the certificate recombines to it
- [x] **The `2θ` row is retained and never published.** The counterexample is executable: two `eqangle`s (`∠(l₁,l₂) = ∠(l₃,l₄)` and `∠(l₁,l₂) = ∠(l₄,l₃)`) entail `2θ₂ − 2θ₁ ≡ 0` and every even multiple of it, and entail **neither** `para` nor `perp`. **Mutation-checked**: normalizing an installed row by its coefficient gcd — what a rational pivot amounts to here — fails exactly those two tests and nothing else
- [x] **Asking does not assert**: a refused question leaves the basis, the rank and the input list untouched. A target that cannot be reduced must not quietly extend the closure on its way to being refused
- [x] Pure Dart, no geometry — the predicate → equation mapping is 152b. `para`/`perp`/Chasles/`para_transitive`/`perp_perp_para`/`para_perp_perp`/`eqangle_transitive` all fall out as row arithmetic and are pinned as such
- [x] Gates: analyze clean, suite **3049** green (3008 + 41)

### 152b — the vocabulary mapped onto it (done)

- [x] **`angle_translation.dart`**: `para` / `perp` / `eqangle` / `coll` in as rows, `para` / `perp` out as facts with certificates. `cong` / `eqratio` are the length system's and every other kind is DD's, each answering false rather than being silently skipped
- [x] **A variable is a point *pair*, not a carrier — the plan had this backwards.** Indexing θ by carrier is wrong twice: two pairs on one carrier do have equal θ, but equal θ is *parallel*, not *identical*, so the implication runs one way only; and the carrier moves when carriers merge, which is Phase 151c's mutable-key problem again. So the variable is a stable pair, and `coll` contributes the θ-equalities its own three pairs license — with exact provenance, since one `coll` states each
- [x] **Incidence does not reduce to the algebra, and the boundary is now pinned.** Given `coll(a,b,c)` and `coll(a,b,d)` the closure holds `θ_ab = θ_ac = θ_bc = θ_ad = θ_bd` and says *nothing* about `θ_cd` — not from weakness but because the pair `cd` names no variable. That `c` and `d` are each on line `ab` is an incidence statement and directions cannot express it; `CarrierIndex` is what knows it. The bridge is applied at **query** time in 152c, not eagerly — eagerly means one variable and one published fact per pair on every line, which is Phase 151b's spelling explosion at a new address
- [x] **`CarrierIndex`'s job here is sharper than indexing**: it tells a parallel from an identity, so `para` is never published about a line and itself. Pairs sharing a point are skipped for the same reason
- [x] **`eqangle` is deliberately not enumerated on output** — pairs of variables are quadratic and quadruples quartic, so publishing every entailed `eqangle` would be worse than the blowup AR exists to remove. DD *asks* instead, which is 152c
- [x] **Measured, and the headline is soundness**: across the five fixtures AR published **51 `para`/`perp` facts and not one failed the numeric screen**. Rules falling out as arithmetic with no rule: `perp_perp_para`, `para_perp_perp`, `para_transitive`, `eqangle_transitive`, Chasles — and **`perp_coll`**, the rule Phase 150 added and Phase 151 could not delete
- [x] **And the other half: AR alone is nearly useless, so the exchange is where the value is.** From hypotheses only it misses 20 of DD's 21 `para`/`perp` on `perp-true-unproved.rgl` and 26 of 29 on `provoleas2.json` — because those come from `midline_para`, `perp_bisector` and the inscribed pair, whose premises AR does not read. Fed DD's *fixpoint*, it yields **+21 beyond DD's 21 on `perp-true-unproved` in 2 ms**, and **+6 on `provoleas2` in 3 ms** — the document DD cannot finish in 30 000 applications and 778 ms. `apatitos-topos` and `no-locus` gain nothing
- [x] **AR's output is not a superset of DD's, by design**: fed the fixpoint it still declines 10 of `perp-true-unproved`'s 21, all of them a line against itself or a pair sharing a point. Recorded so the difference is not read as a miss
- [x] Tests (18) + Gates: analyze clean, suite **3067** green (3049 + 18)

### 152c — one `Prover` facade: the interleaved fixpoint (done)

**152b's measurement made this the phase, not the polish.** AR alone reaches almost nothing; fed DD's fixpoint it doubles the angle facts on `perp-true-unproved` in 2 ms. The value is entirely in the exchange, and it runs both ways — AR's new `para`/`perp` are DD pivots, which derive more, which AR reads again.

- [x] **`prover.dart` — DD and AR as peers over one `FactDatabase`.** A pass is: DD steps under the budget, AR reads everything new, AR publishes what it entails, DD takes the publication into its pivot queue. The run ends when DD is quiescent **and** the last pass published nothing, so it is a loop and not a pipeline. `ProverEngine.absorbExternal` is the one addition the engine needed — sound because its `_facts` mirror *is* the database's list, so what it has not seen is the tail
- [x] **AR's output is screened by the same filter DD screens with**, with no exception made. The algebra is believed; a conclusion that is not numerically true in the diagram means something upstream is wrong, and letting it in would hide that
- [x] **The budget counts DD applications only.** AR's cost is not in the same currency — a pass is a bounded elimination measured in single-digit milliseconds on every fixture — and putting two incomparable things behind one number would make the cap mean nothing
- [x] **An AR step is verified by re-derivation, not by re-multiplication** (`derivation_check.dart`). There is no rule to re-match, so the check builds a closure over the recorded premises *alone* and asks whether they entail the conclusion. Checking the stored certificate would be cheaper and would be checking the engine's arithmetic against itself; this is the same choice the file already makes in restating the matcher rather than sharing it. **Mutation-checked**: dropping the entailment test fails exactly the fabricated-step test
- [x] **Measured against DD alone**, cap 30 000 applications: `perp-true-unproved.rgl` **41 → 93 facts**, still quiescent, 202 → 312 ms, 21 angle steps; `locus3.json` 15 → 24, still quiescent, no slower; `provoleas2.json` 75 → 81 at the cap. `apatitos-topos.rgl` and `no-locus.rgl` unchanged and no slower. **Nothing lost on any fixture** — the exchange's fact set contains DD's
- [x] **Every proof of every angle step verifies**, across all 31 of them — and `Proof.of` walks the whole sub-DAG, so that is the DD steps underneath as well
- [x] **The cost is DD applications, and it is real**: `perp-true-unproved` goes 3 906 → 16 034, because AR's conclusions are pivots. It still reaches quiescence. On a document already at the cap (`provoleas2`) AR gets one look instead of several, which is the honest limitation of a budget shared this way
- [x] Tests (14) + Gates: analyze clean, suite **3081** green (3067 + 14)

## Phase 155 — the tangent hypothesis (open, no dependencies)

## Phase 148 — M-P4d: asking, and the three answers

The panel derives everything and lists what it found — "what follows from this figure". What it cannot do is take a question. JGEX's model is the other way round: state a conclusion, get a proof. This phase adds the question, and it is cheap because DD is *already* not goal-directed — DDAR runs forward to quiescence and then checks membership, so `proofOf` is the second half and only the asking is missing.

**The answer is three-way, and that is the phase's point.** The numeric filter sits beside the prover, so "false" and "unprovable" are separable, and they are very different news:

| answer | means | who says so |
|---|---|---|
| refuted | not a theorem — a perturbation breaks it | `DiagramFilter`, with no run at all |
| unproved | true in the model, past the DD core's reach | membership miss on a *complete* run |
| proved | here is the proof, and `verify()` says certificate | `Proof.of` |

`test/fixtures/perp-true-unproved.rgl` is the middle row, from a user document: right angle at B, D = mid AB, E = mid DB, the perpendicular to CA through E meeting BC at F. `perp(C,D,D,F)` holds in every perturbation and the 44-application fixpoint cannot reach it — **structurally**, since only two of the 23 rules conclude a `perp` (`para_perp_perp` needs a parallel that does not exist here, `perp_bisector` degenerates). The human proof ends in `∠CDF = ∠BDF + ∠BDC`, which *adds* two angles; `eqangle` can only say two angles are equal. That is the AR half of DDAR — **M-P3** — and this fixture is now the thing asking for it.

- [x] **What a selection can phrase** (`lib/domain/prover/questions.dart`, pure): selected objects → point pairs (a selected pair of points is one; a carrier contributes every pair of points `structurallyIncident` on it, as `hypotheses.dart` already does) → the askable questions. Two pairs give `para` / `perp`, plus `cong` when both pairs actually bound a length rather than merely witnessing a direction. Three points give `coll` and the three `midp` readings; four points give `cyclic` and the three pairings of `para` / `perp` / `cong`. `eqangle` / `eqratio` / `simtri` / `contri` are **not** selection-shaped (eight points, or a correspondence a selection cannot express) and are left out, said rather than silently missing. A carrier with fewer than two known points phrases nothing — honest, since DD could do nothing with an unnamed line either
- [x] **A question is a statement, not a spelling — and this is the part that is easy to get wrong.** `para(A,B,C,D)` and `para(A,X,C,D)` are *different facts* even with `A`, `B`, `X` collinear, because the core has no coll-propagation rules (Session 156's note, and the same gap that blocks `perp-true-unproved.rgl`). So a question asked with one witness pair would miss a proof spelled with another. A `ProverQuestion` therefore carries **every** equivalent spelling, and is proved when *any* of them is in the database: which points name a line is the prover's business, not the user's
- [x] **Early exit** (`ProverEngine.runChunked`'s `stopWhen`): with a goal there is no need for quiescence, only for one fact. Checked between chunks, so a run overshoots by less than `chunkBudget`. **Measured on `provoleas2.json`, the document that never reaches quiescence: 1 000 applications and 17.7 ms to answer, against 30 000 and 776.6 ms that still answers nothing** — asking is 44× cheaper than deriving *and* it terminates, which is the whole argument for doing this before the isolate work
- [x] **`ProverNotifier.ask(Predicate)`**: refuted straight from the filter with no run; otherwise run with the goal as the stop condition, reusing a held run when one is already complete at this revision. The answer distinguishes *unproved* from *not yet*: a budget-exhausted run has not shown anything about reachability, and saying "unprovable" there would be a lie
- [x] **Selection-driven UI** in `proof_panel.dart`: with a selection that phrases something, the panel offers those questions as chips *above* the derived list — "what follows from this figure" and "does this hold?" are both useful, and a selection does not say which one the reader wants. Chips name their points, because four selected points phrase three different perpendicularity questions and a row all reading "perpendicular?" would be a coin toss. A proved answer drops into the existing step rows with the existing on-figure emphasis: the proof of an asked question is not a different kind of object from the proof of a listed one. One back affordance, in the header — not at the foot of a proof the reader would have to scroll past
- [x] Tests: `questions.dart` over selections of points, segments, lines and mixtures (including what it refuses to phrase); the early exit reaching the same answer as a full run and costing less on the blowup fixture; the three answers on rigs that produce each — Varignon for *proved*, `perp-true-unproved.rgl` for *unproved*, a false claim for *refuted*; the panel's chips and the three renderings
- [x] Gates: analyze clean, suite **2979** green (2950 + 29), 32 goldens byte-identical, browser gate green (6), `flutter build web --wasm --release` compiles

## Phase 132c — a locus driven by a point on a conic

Phase 132 made a general conic a curve a point can be glued to and left the locus behind: `_SweepDomain.of` asked a `GeoCircle` host for its `CircleEq` and gave up when there was none, so a locus driven by such a point had **no samples at all** — silently absent, which is the harder kind to notice.

- [x] **The domain is the pencil circle the point is already glued on**: `φ = π·x`, cyclic with period 1, one bijection from `[0, π)` onto the whole curve. `pointAt` *is* `pointAtComplex` at a real angle, so the real and complex evaluations are one expression and a detour rejoins the real axis bitwise with nothing kept in step by hand — the property the line and circle arms buy with two parallel formulas each
- [x] **`ConicShape.chartLiftAt` — the pencil value a tracing consumer can take.** `pointAtComplex` is polynomial and homogeneous, so it answers at whatever scale the algebra leaves: **`w = −0.079` at `φ = 0.3`** on `x²/16 + y²/4 = 1`, and *negative*. `PointOnObject.tracedPosition`'s contract is `w` exactly one or exactly zero, because `position` reads x and y straight back without dividing (so a sweep along a diverging arm keeps full precision). Hand over the raw value and every chain member reading the driver's chart position gets it rescaled and, half the time, reflected
- [x] **The drag path had the same hole, and its two sibling arms did not.** `Construction._chartEvaluator`'s conic arm returned `pointAtComplex` directly; the line and circle arms both build `w = Complex.one` by hand. Nothing in the suite moved when it was fixed, and that is the expected result rather than a reassuring one — every chain member in the existing conic rigs is projective, so an arbitrary scale is invisible to them. What it is *not* invisible to is a chart reading: `LineThroughTwoPoints` orients its `LineEq` off `p2 − p1`, and the four kinds Phase 136c named pick their branch off exactly such a direction
- [x] **The ellipse is the class that is swept**, on the gate `extentAlong` already states: an ellipse is the only conic whose curve is bounded. A bounded conic host (an `angularExtent` with no `CircleEq`) is refused for a different reason — an angular extent is a *circle's* span and stands in no relation to a pencil angle. No object reaches that state today, and the guard says so rather than trusting that it stays true
- [x] **Making the circle arm a `circle?` pattern made every arm below it reachable for the first time**, `default:`'s `StateError` included. A `GeoCircle()` arm answers null there now — which is what the circle arm used to do from the inside. Phase 132's lesson, applied on the way past rather than after the crash
- [x] Coverage: **23 tests** — the sweep exists and every sample is on the closed-form traced curve; the domain is one full period with no closing duplicate and the whole curve covered; a spy in the chain sees the driver **on its host conic at every sample** (the normalization, stated as the property it buys); the sample is the driver's own `chartPointAt` bit for bit; the stored parameter and the chain survive; a chain with an `IntersectionPoint` holds one sheet; the four refused classes get null and **no throw**; an extent-claiming conic is refused; the whole path from the toolbar with a `FivePointConic` and a glued tap; and five on `chartLiftAt` itself. Gates: suite **2701** green, analyze clean, 32 goldens byte-identical, browser gate green
- [x] **The hyperbola and the parabola sweep too, on a grid cut at infinity.** `ConicShape.infinityParameters` (the crossings `_breakParameters` already found for `polylines`, published) cuts the pencil circle into the curve's *arcs* — a hyperbola's two branches, a parabola's single arm. Each arc takes an equal share of the domain and `sampleCount` rounds **up** to a multiple of the arc count, so every crossing lands *on* a grid sample. An ellipse has no crossings, so it is one arc from 0 and sweeps `φ = π·x` exactly as before, bit for bit
- [x] **The defect it fixes is not the magnitude, which is what the phase was opened expecting.** Over **200** hyperbolas whose first crossing sweeps 0.13π to 0.23π, a plain uniform pencil grid joins the two branches into one polyline in **200 of 200** — landing a sample exactly on a crossing is measure-zero, so the run never breaks and the locus draws a chord from one branch across the figure to the other. Cut on the crossings: **0 of 200**, and the worst sample falls from **3384** world units to **45**
- [x] **Only one of a hyperbola's two crossings is at a nice angle.** Its base point *is* one of its points at infinity, so that one's asymptote parameter comes out 0 or π/2 and a uniform grid hits it by luck; the second lands wherever the matrix puts it. - [x] **Nothing further was needed for the split.** The crossing sample either leaves the traced point undefined, and `_runs` breaks the branches apart there, or it does not, and the chain genuinely continues through driver-infinity — the full-line host's rule, unchanged, with `hasInfinityWrap` probing the wrap the same way. - [x] **`center` / `halfSpan` mean on an unbounded conic host what they mean on a line, said chart-side**: the driver within `halfSpan` of where it stood when the locus was made (`LocusTool` passes `driver.parameter` as `center`). ## Phase 132d — the pencil angle is an address, and the gesture holds it

Phase 132's stability half. A conic-glued point's stored angle names a point only through `ConicShape`'s canonical frame, and the frame's discrete choices switch as the host moves — the measured 2.7-world-unit jumps. The settlement is `branchIndex`'s, not the sketch the phase was opened with: recompute stays pure and canonical; the drag session carries identity across each switch; the command adopts the net re-expressions. See PLAN §Parameterization.

- [x] **`ConicShape.frameSeeds`** — the base-point candidates the canonical constructor chooses among, recomputed by the same operations so the canonical choice is **bitwise a member** (pinned over all three curve classes). That is what makes "did the frame switch?" an exact element comparison instead of a world-space tolerance
- [x] **`ConicShape.carryParameterFrom`** — hands the angle back *identically* where the canonical frame continues the previous one (the common case pays nothing and drifts nowhere — pinned on the clean rotating near-circle, 200 steps, all bitwise no-ops); at a switch, replays the previous frame on the new matrix and reads the angle back canonically, so the named point does not move (pinned: the switch step stays at the figure's own motion scale). Null at a class change — no frame continues there — and the caller falls back to the point's previous position
- [x] **Why not re-anchor inside `recompute`, recorded so it stays rejected**: unconditional continuation drifts first-order under pure translation (each step projects the old point onto the new curve instead of riding the frame, and the error accumulates over a drag); and any parameter mutation inside recompute is left behind by the locus sweep's restore (`Locus._recompute` restores the *driver's* parameter only), survives `point_coincidence.dart`'s perturb-and-restore, and breaks bitwise undo. - [x] **`_GlueTracker`** in the translate drag session — the `_BranchSnapshot` shape: per preview frame, compares every affected circle-kind host's parameterization frame with the previous frame's and re-expresses each glued parameter across a switch, the polar-angle ↔ pencil-angle boundary included (a host crossing the `CircleEq` projection tolerance flips the parameter's *meaning*; only the position carries). Rollback restores the start parameters bitwise; both single-point and rigid-translation paths are covered
- [x] **`GlueChange` on the commands** — `MoveFreePointCommand.glueChanges` and `TranslateObjectsCommand.glueChanges`, applied before the moves like branch adoptions so one topological pass settles, replayed on apply and undo. A switch-free gesture commits none

## Phase 127 — `RotatedPoint`, and the deferral re-examined

The last piece of Phase 125's per-kind debt that was a **gap** rather than a refusal. The deferral said a CK rotation about a general centre needs an orthonormal frame in the pencil through it, and that the sign of the dual form varies across that pencil so the angle is not uniformly circular. **The second half is true; the first is not** — and the half that is true is about the *centre*, not the pencil. See PLAN §"A rotation is the exponential of one generator".

- [x] **No frame, because the isometries are a Lie group.** The maps preserving the absolute have Lie algebra `{Ω⁻¹K : K antisymmetric}`, every antisymmetric 3×3 is a cross-product `[C]ₓ` with kernel `C`, so the group fixing `C` is `exp(t·Ω*[C]ₓ)` with no basis chosen anywhere. The generator satisfies `N³ = −λ²N`, which closes the exponential into Rodrigues' formula — two matrix products, no series. `ProjTransform.ckRotation`
- [x] **One formula for all three geometries**: expanded under the Euclidean dual `diag(1,1,0)` it reproduces the shipped `ProjTransform.rotation` **entry for entry**, `λ = 1` included, over a spread of centres and angles. Same relationship `harmonicHomology` has to `reflection`. The Euclidean route is kept anyway, exact and cheaper, per the Phase 122/124 rule
- [x] **The sign of `λ²` is the classical trichotomy, and it is where the deferral was right.** `λ²` is the absolute's form at the centre up to sign and scale: positive → interior, and the pencil through an interior point misses the dual conic entirely so its angle measure is elliptic and the rotation is as circular as a Euclidean one; zero → on the absolute, a parabolic horolation with no angle; negative → outside, a boost along the centre's polar whose parameter is a **rapidity**. The last two refuse with the zero map, because `cos`/`sin` there would answer with a *different* isometry rather than an approximate one
- [x] **Two conventions pinned rather than inherited.** The sign of `λ` is free and the stored hyperbolic dual is the adjugate (`−Ω⁻¹`), so a generator taken at face value turns clockwise in one geometry and counter-clockwise in another — handedness is anchored so positive angles are counter-clockwise in the chart in all three. And the centre is **dephased first**: `λ²` scales as `k²`, so an `i`-scaled representative of an ordinary interior centre would fail the realness test and be refused as a boost
- [x] `ProjPoint.dephased` promoted out of `ck_measure.dart`'s private canonicalization, with the Phase 125 exactness note that made it a *phase* division rather than a component division. `_canonical` now delegates, same arithmetic
- [x] `RotatedPoint` wired; per-kind tests for the orbit (every image the same CK distance from the centre — no wrong scale, sign or chart artefact survives that), the two refusals, elliptic's universality, and the Euclidean answer untouched. Suite **2557** green (from 2532), analyze clean, 32 goldens byte-identical
- [x] ~~The macro tools that build on `RotatedPoint`~~ — measured and fixed in **Phase 128** below. Both were wrong, and the claim recorded here ("an equilateral triangle by three CK rotations is genuinely equilateral") was false

## Phase 125 — M-CK2: the tier-2/3 substitutions

The bulk of the milestone: 32 kinds. Taken in the audit's order — the verbatim pole/polar generalizations first, then the genuine re-foundings, then the refusals. **Zero Euclidean behaviour change** throughout.

- [x] `euclidean.dart` → **`metric.dart`**, every function taking an `Absolute`. It was never only Euclidean; it was the Euclidean case of a family
- [x] **Midpoint and angle bisector are the same construction, dually** (PLAN §"Two constructions, one algebra"). A midpoint is the pair harmonic to `{P,Q}` and to the absolute's two points on `PQ`; parameterizing `X(t) = P + tQ`, harmonic to `{0,∞}` forces `M₋ = −M₊`, and harmonic to the absolute's roots forces `t² = ⟨P,P⟩/⟨Q,Q⟩` — so `M± = √⟨Q,Q⟩·P ± √⟨P,P⟩·Q`. Under Euclidean `⟨P,P⟩ = w²` and that reads `w_Q·P ± w_P·Q`: **the affine midpoint, plus the point at infinity in direction `P−Q`** — the external midpoint, whose sitting at infinity is why Euclidean seems to have one. The same algebra in the dual gives `B± = √⟨m,m⟩*·ℓ ± √⟨ℓ,ℓ⟩*·m`, which under `diag(1,1,0)` is exactly the √-unitization `twoLineBisectorOf` already had
- [x] Perpendicularity generalizes verbatim — `perpendicularThrough(P,ℓ) = P ∨ absolute.poleOf(ℓ)`, the audit's structural claim, now a test
- [x] `parallelThrough` **refused** under a proper absolute (zero triple), not approximated
- [x] Two cross-cutting fixes the tests found: **conjugacy is perpendicularity** in every CK geometry, so a vanishing pairing between two *non-isotropic* lines is a right angle whatever the self-pairings' signs (a line and its perpendicular can sit on opposite sides of the absolute, making the product under the root negative); and **every measure removes a global phase first**, because the CK constructions take a square root of a form that is negative inside the absolute and hand back real elements scaled by `i`. The normalization divides by the pivot's unit *phase*, not the pivot — it must be exact, since the perpendicularity test is an exact zero and rounding it to 2.7e-17 lost every right angle it touched
- [x] The eleven `metric.dart` consumer kinds wired, with the absolute threaded through the two template methods (`TriangleCenterPoint.computeCenter`, `RelativeLine.carrierFrom`) rather than each override reaching for it
- [x] **Transforms**: `ProjTransform.harmonicHomology` added, and it reproduces the shipped Euclidean reflection matrix **entry for entry** — the audit's claim that reflection *is* the homology about `(axis, its pole)`, checked rather than asserted; point reflection is the homology about `(centre, its polar)`, ℓ∞ under Euclidean, which is why it reads as a homothety of −1
- [x] **Circles** in `ck_circles.dart`, and they split the way distance splits (PLAN §"A circle follows its distance measure"). `d(C,X) = r` squares to `⟨C,X⟩² = k⟨C,C⟩⟨X,X⟩`, i.e. `K = ⟨R,R⟩(ΩC)(ΩC)ᵀ − ⟨C,R⟩²Ω` — the pencil through the absolute and the doubled polar of the centre, hence **bitangent**. Under Euclidean that whole pencil collapses to multiples of ℓ∞, so `circles.dart` is a *companion*, not a special case. A centre on the absolute is a **horocycle**, not a degeneracy. `Arc`/`Sector` refused: the carrier generalizes, the angular extent is a chart parameterization
- [x] The five re-foundings, and **three of them turned out to generalize** because their definitions are constructions rather than formulas: the centroid is the medians' common point (CK midpoints), the incentre the internal bisectors' (CK bisector), the inscribed circle centred there through the foot of a perpendicular. `FixedRadiusCircle` gets `ckCircleWithRadius`. Refused with reasons at the site: homothety and translation (a similarity that is not an isometry does not exist in CK — Wallis), rotation (needs an orthonormal frame in the pencil, whose form changes sign across it), Apollonius and bifocal conics, the radical axis, the focal conic, the circle centre (a generalized eigenproblem for the pencil `{K, Ω}`), segment-ratio points
- [x] `IntersectionPoint`'s circular-point filter made absolute-aware, and threaded **all at once** because `branchIndex` addresses the *filtered* order — a caller left on the default lands its points in a different address space, the Phase 120c failure from a new direction. One caller knowingly stays on the default and says so: the tool layer has no construction to read a kernel from, which is correct only while the codec refuses non-Euclidean documents. **Phase 126 must carry the kernel into the tools before lifting that refusal**
- [x] **The `recompute` default is kept, and that reverses Phase 124's schedule on purpose.** The Phase 121 precedent says a migration default inverts into a trap once the migration ends — but the trap it described was a kind *silently inheriting* a wrong value, and that cannot happen here: the parameter is supplied by `Construction` at every production call site, so the default only ever applies in tests that mean Euclidean. The risk that remains is a new kind whose `recompute` ignores the parameter, and a signature cannot catch that — only behaviour can. `ck_kind_coverage_test` does: over the corpus at three layouts, every kind must change its value or withdraw it. That is a strictly stronger gate than the one removing the default would buy, and it costs ~140 test call sites less churn
- [x] ~~**A coverage gate, not a proof.**~~ — the proof it was missing is `ck_isometry_invariance_test` (Phase 131), and the coverage gate's own blind spot is what it found: a kind with *one* of two ingredients substituted still moves, so `ck_kind_coverage_test` passed `Orthocenter` while one of its altitudes was Euclidean. Equivariance is not a substitute for per-kind correctness — a kind computing the wrong *equivariant* thing would pass — but it rules out the family of defects per-kind tests were being written one at a time to catch

## Phase 124 — M-CK1: measurement, and the half of it that is not a cross-ratio

PLAN §M-CK said the tier-4 kinds are "re-founded on cross-ratio, with Euclidean as the degenerate case that reproduces today's numbers". **Measured first: that is true of angle and false of distance**, which is the classical Cayley–Klein classification arriving as a code constraint — Euclidean has *elliptic angle measure* and *parabolic distance measure*.

- [x] **Angle unifies, at the bilinear-form level rather than through logs.** `cos θ = ⟨ℓ,m⟩*/√(⟨ℓ,ℓ⟩*·⟨m,m⟩*)` against the *dual* absolute is Laguerre's cross-ratio formula with the logs cleared, and under `diag(1,1,0)` it is `(a₁a₂+b₁b₂)/(|n₁||n₂|)` — the textbook Euclidean formula, verbatim. Measured: agrees with the chart route to **7.4e-13** over 20k random line pairs
- [x] **Distance does not unify, and cannot.** A line meets the Euclidean absolute (the *doubled* ℓ∞) in one point twice, and a cross-ratio with a repeated point is identically 1, so the log is identically 0 for every pair of points. Euclidean distance is not a cross-ratio at any constant — recovering it needs the constant to diverge as the absolute degenerates. So distance **dispatches on the measure** where angle substitutes: parabolic (Euclidean, the chart route it already uses), hyperbolic (`cosh d = |⟨P,Q⟩|/√(⟨P,P⟩⟨Q,Q⟩)`), elliptic (the same with `cos`). Verified against `artanh(r)` and against the cross-ratio route to the last digit
- [x] Thread the absolute into `recompute`, per Phase 123's decision — **defaulted to Euclidean while the migration runs**, which is the pattern Phases 106–121 already used for the projective accessors and then removed. Scheduled for removal at the end of Phase 125, when all 38 kinds take it; `Construction` passes `kernel.absolute` from the start, so the default is scaffolding at the declarations and never a live fallback
- [x] `Absolute` gains the bilinear pairings (`pairPoints`, `pairLines`) the quadratic forms of Phase 123 polarize to; `lib/domain/projective/ck_measure.dart` holds the two measures and names the three measure kinds
- [x] `VertexAngle` and `LineAngle`: the CK formula becomes the *definition*, with the exact Euclidean route retained as its specialization — the Phase 122 circle-fast-path rule, for the same reason (it is exact, cheaper, and keeps `expect(angle, 90)` reading exactly 90). Agreement proven by test rather than by routing. The marker's vertex and start direction stay chart quantities, like a measurement's anchor
- [x] `DistanceMeasurement` on the three measures. `LengthMeasurement` and `AreaMeasurement` are **integrals**, not substitutions (hyperbolic circumference is `2π·sinh r`, and a hyperbolic triangle's area is its angular defect) — deliberately **not** implemented here, and undefined rather than wrong under a proper absolute. `SlopeMeasurement` is Euclidean by definition and goes undefined with the others
- [x] Gates: **zero Euclidean behaviour change** — suite **2438** green, analyze clean, **32 goldens byte-identical**, perf gate PASS at 1% / 7% of budget with unchanged checksums. Every non-Euclidean path is reachable only from tests this phase, since the codec still refuses the two other metrics. Also added: `GeoAngle.measure` beside `angle`, because a marker is not a measure — the arc is still drawn between the same two chords while the number is the CK angle
